All records screening

The approved AI model screens every record twice, as two independent readings. When one reading excludes a record and the other keeps it, that is a conflict for you to decide. Records you voted on in the pilot keep your decision. Yes and Maybe go forward to full-text review.

    Conflicts and Human check

    Resolve every conflict: both readings are shown. Then check exclusions both readings agree on, without seeing the AI first; that is where an AI error loses a study. Your decision replaces the AI's. A random check bounds how often AI exclusions are wrong; it cannot prove that no relevant study was missed.

    Method and research references

    All records screening: the approved model screens every record twice (pass 2 presents the record first and the criteria in reverse order); a record one pass excludes and the other keeps is a conflict the reviewer resolves; pilot votes stand; Human check reviews AI exclusions blind to the AI (a random 300 by default, or all), with an exact binomial 95% upper bound on the share of AI exclusions that are wrong. This bounds the error rate of exclusions and does not prove that no relevant study was missed.

    Pilot screening: one local AI model and the reviewer screen the same random sample independently; the AI judges each criterion and may exclude only with an exact quotation; disagreements and the reviewer's notes become worked examples for the next round. Agreement is measured on forward (Yes or Maybe) versus exclude. The recommended standard (no false exclusions; Cohen's κ ≥ 0.6 or agreement ≥ 75%) follows common agreement conventions and the JBI pilot rule; it is a guide for the reviewer's approval, not a validated stopping rule or a guarantee of recall.

    • Cochrane Handbook v6.5.1, Chapter 4 §4.6.4 — pilot the eligibility criteria on a sample of reports before screening.
    • Flemyng et al. (2025), Cochrane, Campbell, JBI and CEE position statement on AI in evidence synthesis — pilot or calibrate an AI tool within the review, keep human oversight and report its use.
    • Tran et al. (2024), Ann Intern Med — high-sensitivity LLM screening rules.
    • Cao et al. (2025), Ann Intern Med — optimised criteria-based prompts reach about 98% sensitivity; zero-shot prompts much lower.
    • Xie et al. (2026), J Evid Based Med — meta-analysis: few-shot or reasoning prompts improve sensitivity.
    • Affengruber, Gartlehner et al. (2026), BMC Med Res Methodol — false exclusions rise when abstracts lack information; uncertain records go forward.
    • Garritty et al. (2024), BMJ: updated Cochrane rapid review methods guidance — when one person screens, a second checks all exclusions.
    • Gartlehner et al. (2025), Cochrane Rapid Reviews Methods Group position statement on AI — do not fully automate a step; use AI with human checking of exclusions.
    • Callaghan and Müller-Hansen (2020), Syst Rev — statistical checks of missed records in a random sample of unscreened or excluded records.
    • PRISMA 2020 explanation and elaboration, item 8 — report the automation tool, its role and how its risk of missed studies was assessed.
    • Covidence: Maybe votes — uncertain citations advance to full text.
